# Whole-Wheat Ajwain Baati — Bread Maker Dough + OTG

**Type:** weekday-dish  
**Fridge life:** 2 days; suitable through Day 3 breakfast when refrigerated after cooling 30-40 min · **Freezer-safe:** No, because cooked wheat breads/rotis are not freezer-approved  
**Active time:** 25-30 min · **Makes:** 15 medium baati = 5 portions of 3 baati for 1 person  
**Uses prepped components:** none  
**Source:** own  

*Style: firm whole-wheat baati with a crisp exterior, cooked completely in the Borosil 60L OTG. Light ajwain flavour. Three medium baati make one meal portion.*

## Ingredients

| Ingredient | Quantity |
| --- | ---: |
| Atta | 500 g |
| Ghee | 70 g, about 5 tbsp, melted but not hot |
| Ajwain | 1¼ tsp, lightly crushed |
| Salt | 1½ tsp |
| Baking soda | ⅛ tsp, optional |
| Water | 200 ml initially |
| Extra water | Up to 25 ml, only as required during kneading |
| Extra atta | 1-2 tbsp, only if dough becomes too soft |
| Ghee for serving | 1-2 tsp per meal, optional |

## Process — American Micronic Bread Maker Dough

1. Add to the bread-maker pan in this order: **200 ml water**, **70 g melted ghee**, **500 g atta**, **1½ tsp salt**, **1¼ tsp lightly crushed ajwain**, and optional **⅛ tsp baking soda**.
2. Select the **Poori/Puri dough cycle**, not the roti dough cycle. Baati needs a **firmer dough**, closer to poori dough than soft chapati dough.
3. Let the machine knead and inspect the dough after approximately **4-5 minutes**.
4. The correct dough should collect into **one firm, smooth ball**. It should feel noticeably firmer than regular roti dough and should not smear or stick heavily to the bread-maker pan.
5. If loose dry atta remains and the dough cannot form a ball, add extra water **1 tsp at a time**, waiting **45-60 seconds** between additions.
6. Use no more than approximately **225 ml total water** unless your atta is unusually absorbent. Do not make the dough soft merely because all the water has not been used.
7. If the dough becomes sticky or soft, first let it knead for another **1-2 minutes**. If still sticky, add extra atta **1 tbsp at a time**.
8. Allow approximately **10-12 minutes total kneading**. Once the dough is smooth, firm and evenly hydrated, stop the machine if its programme continues into a long resting/proofing stage. Baati does not need yeast-style proofing.
9. Remove the dough, cover it and rest for **10 minutes**.

## Process — Shaping and Borosil 60L OTG

1. During the dough rest, preheat the OTG at **200°C for 15 minutes** with **both upper and lower rods ON**. Keep the rack at the **middle level**.
2. After resting, lightly knead the dough by hand for **20-30 seconds**.
3. Divide it into **15 equal portions**. Each dough portion should be approximately **50-55 g**.
4. Roll each portion firmly between your palms into a smooth round baati. Try to keep all 15 approximately the same size so they bake evenly.
5. Make a **small, shallow thumb indentation** in the centre of each baati. Do not make a hole through it and do not flatten it significantly.
6. Place the baati on the OTG baking tray, leaving approximately **2-3 cm space** between them. The 60L OTG should accommodate all 15 in one batch if arranged with enough airflow.
7. Place the tray on the middle rack and bake at **200°C for 18-20 minutes**.
8. Remove the tray and carefully **turn every baati over**.
9. Bake again at **200°C for 15-18 minutes**.
10. Check one baati after approximately **35 minutes total baking**. Break or cut one open. The centre should be completely cooked and dry, with **no sticky or raw-looking atta**.
11. If the centres are cooked but the outside is still pale, bake for another **3-5 minutes at 210°C**, watching closely.
12. Expected total OTG time is approximately **35-40 minutes**, depending on actual baati size and your OTG's heating behaviour.
13. Remove from the OTG. For the portion being eaten immediately, crack **3 hot baati** slightly and add **1-2 tsp ghee** if desired.
14. Keep the baati intended for storage **plain rather than soaking them in ghee**. Add serving ghee only after reheating.

## Portioning and Storage

- **Day 1 lunch:** 3 fresh baati + lehsun chutney + curd.
- **Day 1 dinner:** 3 baati + dal + lehsun chutney.
- **Day 2 lunch:** 3 baati + curd + lehsun chutney.
- **Day 2 dinner:** 3 baati + dal + lehsun chutney.
- **Day 3 breakfast:** remaining 3 baati, preferably with fresh curd or lehsun chutney.
- After removing the **3 baati for Day 1 lunch**, cool the remaining **12 baati to warm for only 30-40 minutes**.
- Divide the cooled baati into separate portions where practical, transfer to airtight containers and refrigerate immediately. Do not leave even the Day 1 dinner portion outside until evening.
- Day 3 breakfast is approximately the **2-day point** from the morning cook, so use those final baati at breakfast rather than carrying them farther into Day 3.
- For reheating, put only the **3 baati being eaten** on a tawa or in a kadai over **low heat**.
- Cover and heat for approximately **6-8 minutes**, turning every **1-2 minutes**, until piping hot through the centre.
- If the refrigerated baati seems particularly dry, sprinkle approximately **½ tsp water around the hot tawa/kadai**, immediately cover for **1-2 minutes**, then uncover and heat briefly to restore the outside texture.
- Add serving ghee only after the baati is completely reheated.
- Do not cool and refrigerate an already reheated portion again.
- Do not freeze the baati.
